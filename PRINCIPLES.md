# Principles

DUBSAR is guided by evidence and authority boundaries, not by claims of AI autonomy.

## 1. Humans retain protected authority

Agents may inspect, explain and propose. Protected business decisions remain attributable to an authorized human.

## 2. Agents do not self-validate

One agent cannot make another agent's output authoritative merely by agreeing with it. Role separation, deterministic controls and human decisions remain distinct.

## 3. Audit before continuous governance

An organization should understand its workflows, evidence, owners and limitations before installing continuous policy enforcement.

## 4. Evidence is bounded

Every audit identifies its sources, time window, scope, digest, completeness and unavailable reads.

## 5. Missing evidence is not success

An absent or unreadable source can make a control not evaluable. It must never become an implicit pass.

## 6. Declarations are not verification

DUBSAR distinguishes what a tool or model claims from what an identified deterministic evaluator checked.

## 7. Deterministic state surrounds probabilistic work

Identity, rule selection, digests, state transitions, idempotence and Human Gates belong to deterministic components.

## 8. Findings keep their provenance

A material finding remains connected to the Rule Pack, evidence snapshot, source references and review decision that produced it.

## 9. Connectors remain replaceable

No connector owns DUBSAR rules or canonical decisions. Third-party changes must degrade explicitly, not change meaning silently.

## 10. Fail closed at protected boundaries

When required identity, evidence, policy or approval is unavailable, a protected action stops.

Fail-closed behavior remains targeted; harmless reads should not be blocked merely to look strict.

## 11. The portal is designed for users

The planned portal is intended to provide scope, progress, findings, evidence,
human review and reports without requiring users to understand internal
repositories or agent plumbing. It is coming soon and remains under
development and validation; it is not currently available for public use.

## 12. Future installed architecture remains undecided

Continuous governance or installed components may be considered later under a
separate scope. Their architecture, data placement, distribution, support and
licensing remain undecided and are not promised.

## 13. One authority model across evidence sources

Current and future evidence sources should preserve the same distinction
between facts, analysis and human authority. This principle does not select or
promise a future integration architecture.

## 14. Public and private boundaries stay explicit

Public documentation and any separately published material remain separate
from the private Core, private implementation, confidential evidence and trust
material.

## 15. Public claims follow proof

An interactive mockup, API path, deterministic test, user E2E and production deployment are different achievements.

DUBSAR names the boundary that was actually proved.

## 16. Regulation informs controls without creating a legal claim

Rule Packs may map to governance needs such as traceability and human oversight. DUBSAR does not issue legal advice or certify regulatory compliance.

## Summary

```text
Bound the scope.
Preserve the evidence.
Apply explicit controls.
Separate agent output from authority.
Keep limitations visible.
Let the right human decide.
```
